## 1. Who to engage

A professional with **all** of the following:
- native Arabic, with Tunisian legal and administrative Arabic (JORT style)
- professional French
- experience with legal or banking texts

Typical profiles:
- a sworn translator (traducteur assermenté / مترجم محلّف)
- a legal translator who has worked for banks or administrations

**Two reviewers are better than one:** one for Arabic and one for French. If budget allows only one, Arabic is the priority, because most drafts were written in French or English first.

## 2. What to review

- **Workbook (one row per text):** [../data/native-review-workbook.csv](../data/native-review-workbook.csv). It has 17 items, with priority, audience and the columns the reviewer fills in.
- **Reference glossary:** [arabic-legal-terminology.md](arabic-legal-terminology.md). The terms were verified against the official Arabic texts of BCT Circular 2022-08 and Law 2004-63.
- **Rule for the reviewer:** where the regulation uses a term, **keep the regulation's term** (e.g. شكوى, الموفّق البنكي, وصل استلام), even if a more common word exists. Common words (e.g. شكاية) may appear only as explanatory synonyms.

## 3. Review method

1. Read the glossary first.
2. For each workbook row, check:
   - **register**: formal, plain, respectful
   - **terminology**: matches the glossary
   - **meaning**: identical across FR and AR
   - **legal completeness**: for the consent and notice rows R-004 to R-006, all 12 information points required by Law 2004-63 Art. 31 are present
3. Write proposed changes directly in the `proposed_changes` column. Use tracked changes in the source document for long texts.
4. Mark each row as `approved`, `changed` or `open` (needs counsel).
5. Return the workbook. CIVORA applies the changes and records E-13 as met only when **all Critical and High rows are `approved` or `changed`**.
