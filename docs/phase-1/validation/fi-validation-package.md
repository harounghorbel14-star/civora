## 0. Rules for the founder and interviewers

1. **Never fabricate or "fill in" evidence.** An interview counts only if it took place, notes were written within 24 hours, and consent is recorded.
2. **No customer data.** Do not ask for, accept or record any complaint content or personal data of an institution's customers. Anonymised, aggregate figures only.
3. **No official claims.** CIVORA is independent, with no relationship with the BCT, the banking mediator or any authority. Say so in every first contact.
4. **No selling.** The first conversation is research. Pricing questions come at the end, as hypotheses.
5. **Interviewee data.** Names and contact details of interviewees are personal data (Law 2004-63). Keep them in the founder's private contact system, **not in this repository**. The repository holds only coded identifiers. CIVORA's own research processing must be declared to the INPDP (Art. 7) once the company exists. That is a founder action.
6. **Native-language review (E-13)** of the Arabic and French texts before sending.

## 3. Interview questionnaire (45–60 min)

It extends the 10-question guide of §8.12. Questions in **bold** are mandatory for the interview to count toward E-01.

### A. Context (5 min)
1. **Your role and the complaint structure:** size, reporting line, created when? (Art. 4)
2. Approximate **annual number of complaints** (as reported in the BCT statement, if they can share a range). How many service and information requests on top?

### B. Current process (15 min)
3. **Walk me through a recent complaint from receipt to closure.** Where does it get stuck?
4. Which **channels** do you receive complaints on (email, online form, branch, phone, post, social media)? Which is largest?
5. **How do you generate acknowledgements with a date and reference (Art. 8)?** Automatically or manually?
6. **How do you track the 15-working-day limit (Art. 8)?** What share closes on time? How do you know? *(Ask for a range; do not ask for internal reports.)*
7. How do you write, approve and check **reasons for rejections**?
8. How do customers learn the status of their complaint?
9. How do you separate **complaints** from **service or information requests** (Art. 2)?

### C. Regulatory reporting and control (10 min)
10. **How long does it take to prepare the annual BCT statement ROGS760 (or RAA10/RAA20 for payment institutions)?** Who does it? From which system?
11. How do you prepare the annual **board report** (Art. 10) and the **internal-control chapter** (Art. 11)?
12. When was the last **internal audit** of the complaint system (Art. 12)? Any findings you can share in general terms?
13. Has the BCT inspected complaint handling at your institution? *(Only if comfortable; no details required.)*
14. How do you handle **mediator** files: compiling, deadlines, correspondence?

### D. Tools and incumbents (10 min)
15. **Which tool(s) do you use today?** In-house, core-banking or CRM module, help-desk SaaS, spreadsheet. When were they introduced? Was that because of 2022-08?
16. **Satisfaction with the current tool, 1–5**, and the top three gaps.
17. Have you looked at replacing it? What alternatives did you consider, and at what price?

### E. Requirements (5 min)
18. **Hosting and security conditions for a vendor**: Tunisian hosting, on-premises, audits, certifications, BCT notification.
19. Languages required for agents and customers (Arabic, French, both).
20. Integrations that would be necessary (core banking, CRM, SSO, SMS, email).

### F. Buying, budget and next steps (10 min)
Use the procurement questionnaire (§7) and the willingness-to-pay questionnaire (§8).

21. **Who would decide, who would pay, who could block?** (buyer framework, §6)
22. **Would you take part in a 3-month sandbox design-partner programme on synthetic data?** Paid / unpaid / LOI only / no. (Commitment ladder, §11.)

## 7. Procurement and purchasing-process questionnaire

1. For software of this size (indicatively 50,000–200,000 TND per year), what is the procedure: direct order, consultation, or formal tender?
2. What are the thresholds, and who approves at each level?
3. For **state-owned institutions**: do public-procurement rules (TUNEPS) apply to this purchase?
4. Is there a **vendor-risk / outsourcing assessment**? What does it require? (Security questionnaire, financial statements, references, insurance, certifications such as ISO 27001.)
5. Would a SaaS hosted by a third party require you to **inform the BCT before signing** (Law 2016-48 Art. 81)? Who handles that? How long does it take in practice?
6. Hosting: is SaaS in a Tunisian data centre acceptable, or must it run on-premises? Is a labelled provider required?
7. Does any budget exist for complaint handling in 2027? When is the 2027 budget fixed?
8. Typical time from first meeting to signed contract for a comparable purchase.
9. Payment terms: invoicing frequency, payment delay, withholding tax. *(These feed the working-capital assumption in [20](../20-updated-financial-assumptions.md) §20.7.)*
10. Standard contract: the institution's template or the vendor's? Language: French or Arabic?
11. References: do you require Tunisian references before buying? How many?
12. Would a **paid sandbox** design-partner agreement need the same procedure? Or would an LOI be easier?

## 8. Willingness-to-pay questionnaire

Ask **after** the problem discussion, never as an opener. Prices are **experimental hypotheses** (P1-D9).

1. What do you spend today on complaint handling tools: licences, maintenance, internal IT time? A range is fine.
2. How many people handle complaints full-time or part-time? How many branch staff log complaints?
3. **Price sensitivity**, for a solution that meets your stated needs. Use a Van Westendorp-style question set, in TND per month for the whole institution:
   - At what price would it be **so cheap** you would doubt its quality?
   - At what price would it be **a bargain**?
   - At what price would it start to feel **expensive**, though still worth considering?
   - At what price would it be **too expensive** to consider?
4. **Preferred pricing model:** flat per institution / per agent seat / per complaint volume / licence + maintenance (on-premises).
5. **Tier reaction:** show the three experimental tiers from [19](../19-commercial-and-pricing-strategy.md) §19.2:
   - Starter 1,500–2,500 TND/month
   - Professional 4,000–7,000 TND/month
   - Enterprise 8,000–15,000 TND/month, or a licence of 100–180k TND/year

   Ask: which fits? Would you buy it, maybe, or not?
6. **Implementation fee** of 20–40k TND, one-off: acceptable / only if included / not acceptable.
7. **Paid sandbox design-partner fee** (indicative, to test): acceptable / only free / LOI only.
8. Budget timing: could money be committed in 2027? Which quarter?
9. What would make you pay more? (e.g. ROGS760 export, on-premises option, Arabic customer portal, core-banking integration)

**Analysis:**
- Plot the four price curves per segment only when there are **≥ 5 answers per segment**.
- Below that, report the raw ranges.
- E-04 is met when **≥ 5 institutions** give budget ranges or tier choices and **≥ 2 sign** paid design-partner agreements.

## 10. Customer pain-point scoring framework

**Pains P1–P10** (hypotheses linked to obligations):

| ID | Pain hypothesis | Linked obligation |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Missed or uncertain 15-working-day deadlines | Art. 8 |
| P2 | Manual acknowledgement and referencing | Art. 8, 9 |
| P3 | Inconsistent or weak rejection reasons | Art. 8 |
| P4 | Mixing complaints with requests in statistics | Art. 2 |
| P5 | Manual compilation of ROGS760 / RAA statements | Art. 14 |
| P6 | Board report and internal-control chapter take days | Art. 10, 11 |
| P7 | Audit evidence is hard to produce | Art. 12 |
| P8 | Customers cannot see status, so they call or escalate | Art. 7, good practice |
| P9 | Mediator files take long to compile | Art. 13 |
| P10 | Hosting or language limits of current tool | Law 2004-63 Art. 52; customer needs |

**Score each pain per interview:**
- **Frequency F:** 0 never, 1 yearly, 2 monthly, 3 weekly or more.
- **Severity S:** 0 none, 1 minor, 2 costly (hours lost or rework), 3 critical (regulatory finding, audit issue, customer escalation).
- **Workaround W:** 0 satisfactory tool, 1 tolerable workaround, 2 poor workaround, 3 none.
- **Regulatory exposure R:** 1 if the pain relates to an explicit article, else 0.

**Pain score** = F × S × (1 + R) + W. The maximum is 21.

**Thresholds (per pain, across interviews):**
- A pain is **validated** if the median score is ≥ 8 among ≥ 8 interviewed institutions **and** ≥ 5 institutions score it ≥ 10.
- **H-R1 is confirmed** if ≥ 3 pains are validated, including at least one of P1, P5 or P6.

## 11. Demand validation methodology

### 11.1 Hypotheses (§8.12 plus two new ones)

| ID | Hypothesis | Confirm if | Refute if |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-R1 | Complaint handling under BCT 2022-08 is a recurring operational pain | Per §10 thresholds | < 2 pains validated |
| H-R2 | Payment institutions and leasing/factoring companies lack dedicated complaint tooling | ≥ 4 of 7 use email, spreadsheets or a generic tool | Most are equipped |
| H-R3 | Tunisian hosting / on-premises and Arabic/French interfaces are "must-have" | ≥ 5 institutions rate them must-have | Indifferent |

| H-R5 | Institutions will join a paid sandbox programme | ≥ 2 sign (L4) | None |
| **H-R6 (new)** | Existing 2022-08 tooling (introduced around 2023) is **replaceable** | ≥ 5 banks rate satisfaction ≤ 3/5 **and** are open to replacement within 24 months | > 70% satisfied, or locked into multi-year contracts |
| **H-R7 (new)** | Regulatory reporting (ROGS760 / RAA10/RAA20; board report; internal-control chapter) is a **buying trigger** | ≥ 5 institutions spend ≥ 2 person-days a year on it and want automation | Fully automated already |

### 11.3 Procedure and bias controls

1. **Weeks 1–2:**
   - Native review of the emails (E-13).
   - Prepare the private contact list (roles, not names, in the repository).
   - Send to 15 institutions in waves of 5.
   - Follow up after 7 days.
   - Use a reserve institution when a target declines.
2. **Weeks 2–8 — interviews:**
   - Two CIVORA people per interview where possible.
   - Problem questions before any solution mention.
   - No leading questions; ask for past examples, not opinions about the future.
   - The prototype is shown only at the end, if at all, and labelled "synthetic prototype".
3. **Within 24 hours:**
   - Notes completed.
   - Pain scores by each interviewer, separately.
   - Reconciliation.
   - Evidence-log row added.
4. **Week 8–9 — synthesis:**
   - Pain score table.
   - Hypothesis verdicts.
   - Buyer maps.
   - Price ranges.
   - Competitor table.
   - Disconfirming evidence reported with equal weight.
