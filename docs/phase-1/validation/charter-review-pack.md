## 1. Self-check of the 12 commitments against Law 2004-63 (official text read [S168][S182])

| # | Commitment (short) | Legal check | Finding | Proposed change (for review) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Essential services free | No legal rule; business commitment | Consistent | None |
| 2 | Data not for sale | Art. 17 (no tying of a service to consent to other uses); Art. 12 (purpose limitation) | Consistent; stricter than the law | None |
| 3 | Health data for care only | Art. 62–65: listed grounds; processing **only by physicians or persons bound by professional secrecy** (Art. 63); INPDP authorisation to communicate | Consistent in substance; incomplete | Add: "…and handled only by professionals bound by medical or professional secrecy." |
| 4 | Data stays under the country's law; "never moved abroad without a lawful basis" | **Art. 52: INPDP authorisation is required in all cases**; Art. 51 adequacy; Art. 50 prohibition where public security or vital interests are at stake | **Weaker than the law**: "lawful basis" suggests other bases suffice | Reword: "…never moved abroad unless the law allows it and the competent authority has authorised it (in Tunisia: the INPDP)." |
| 5 | Minimisation, retention, deletion | Art. 10–11 (purpose, necessity, accuracy); deletion when no longer needed | Consistent | None |
| 6 | "You can see who looked" wherever CIVORA runs a service | Art. 19 requires access logs. The **right of access does not apply** to processing by public bodies acting for public security, defence or criminal prosecution (Art. 53–56). For bank customers, the institution is the controller | **Over-commitment risk.** CIVORA cannot promise this where the controller's legal framework excludes it, and Workspace RF does not show access logs to bank customers | Reword: "Wherever the institution's legal framework allows it, CIVORA gives you a view of who accessed your data, when and why. CIVORA offers this feature to every institution it serves." *Product implication if kept as is:* add a customer access-log view to Workspace RF (not in the MVP) |
| 7 | Real consent, withdrawable | **Art. 27: express and written consent**, withdrawable at any time; Art. 31 information duties | Consistent; could cite "written" | Add "…in writing or by an equivalent recorded means." |
| 8 | AI explains, people decide | No AI statute identified [U] | Consistent with Phase 0 §11.9 | None |
| 9 | Never pretend to be the government | Master-prompt rule | Consistent | None |
| 10 | Security; prompt incident information "as the law requires" | Art. 18–19; Decree-law 2023-17 Art. 17–20 (immediate notification to the national contact point, where applicable) | Consistent | None |
| 11 | Nobody left behind (counter, mandate) | No legal rule | Consistent | None |
| 12 | Annual transparency report including **requests from authorities** | Some laws may restrict disclosure of authority requests (e.g. Decree-law 2022-54; criminal procedure). Counsel question C8 | **Legal risk not assessed** | Add "…to the extent the law allows." |

**Operational finding (affects the whole Charter and all INPDP filings):**
- In October 2026, a Tunisian digital-rights forum publicly stated that the **INPDP's activities are stopped**, while its legal framework remains in force [P][S198].
- The INPDP website (www.inpdp.tn) could not be reached from the research environment on 18 Nov 2026: the network proxy reported the host as unreachable.
- If true, declarations (Art. 7) and authorisations (Art. 15, 52, 63) may be impossible to obtain in practice.
- This is an urgent counsel question: **A4** in the [counsel brief](counsel-engagement-brief.md).

### 2.2 Letter (FR)

> **Objet :** Demande d'avis — projet de Charte de protection des données d'une start-up tunisienne
>
> Madame, Monsieur,
>

Elle deviendra contraignante (contrats, statuts, code de conduite) après sa publication.
