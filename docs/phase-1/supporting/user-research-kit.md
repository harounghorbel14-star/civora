# User Research Kit (Phase 1 supporting material; sections numbered 8.x from v1.0)

Everything needed to run Phase 1 field research. **Claude cannot conduct interviews.** The founder/team runs these sessions, and the outputs replace the hypotheses in Sections 1 and 4.

## 8.1 Research questions and hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Evidence that would confirm | Evidence that would refute |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Missing/contradictory information causes most repeat visits | ≥ 50% of citizens recall a repeat visit caused by a missing document or wrong information | Repeat visits mainly caused by other factors (system downtime, staff absence) |
| H2 | Citizens will trust an *unofficial* assistant if it cites official sources | ≥ 60% say they would use it and check the source; trust rises when citations are shown (A/B in usability tests) | Majority distrust any non-government channel regardless of citations |
| H3 | "Where is my file?" is a top frustration for citizens and a top workload for officers | Both groups rank status visibility in their top 3 | Low priority for either group |
| H4 | Families accept formal, revocable mandates for elderly relatives | ≥ 60% of caregivers and ≥ 50% of older adults find it acceptable | Strong preference for informal sharing of credentials, or cultural rejection |
| H5 | Officers lack tools to prioritise and verify completeness | Officers describe paper or ad-hoc tools; no SLA visibility | Existing systems already provide this |
| H6 | Diaspora users' top needs are remote guidance and consular appointments | Diaspora interviewees rank these top 2 | Other needs dominate (e.g. property, taxes) |
| H7 | Banks/payment institutions will pay for locally hosted e-KYC orchestration with evidence packs | ≥ 3 of 10 B2B interviewees state budget and timeline; ≥ 2 agree to a paid pilot | Satisfied with current vendors; no budget |
| H8 | Derja (incl. Arabizi) is the preferred way to ask questions in writing for many users | ≥ 40% choose Derja or Arabizi when asking the Guide in tests | Users prefer MSA/French for "official" topics |

## 8.3 Research ethics and data protection

- **Informed consent** before every session (Section 8.5). Participants can stop at any time and withdraw their data.
- **Minimum data:**
  - first name or pseudonym
  - age band
  - region
  - digital confidence
  - **No ID numbers, no health details beyond what the participant volunteers.** Do not record such details if volunteered.
- **Recordings** only with explicit consent. Store on encrypted devices or storage located in Tunisia. **Delete within 90 days** after anonymised notes are made.
- **No cloud transcription outside Tunisia** unless the participant consents and the recording is anonymised. Add a question to counsel on whether this research processing requires an INPDP declaration.
- **Incentive:** a modest voucher (e.g. 30–50 TND [A]), identical for all participants and not conditional on answers.
- **Safeguarding:** for older or vulnerable participants, offer a trusted companion. Never demonstrate "mandates" using participants' real relatives' data.
- **Civil servants** participate in a personal capacity unless their institution formally agrees. Never ask them to show real citizen files.

## 8.4 Screener (citizens)

1. In which governorate (or country, if abroad) do you live? → quota.
2. What is your age band? (18–29 / 30–44 / 45–59 / 60+) → quota.
3. In the last 12 months, have you done any administrative procedure (municipality, civil status, passport/ID, CNAM/CNSS/CNRPS, school/university)? **Must be yes.**
4. How do you usually do these procedures? (in person / online / someone helps me / mix)
5. How comfortable are you using a smartphone for official tasks? (1–5) → digital confidence quota.
6. Do you help an older or dependent relative with procedures? (yes/no) → caregiver quota.
7. Do you use any accessibility features (screen reader, large text)? → disability quota.
8. Do you work for a public administration or an IT company in this sector? → **Exclude** from citizen sample (conflict of interest), unless recruiting officers.
9. Which languages are you comfortable reading for official topics? (Arabic / French / English) → ensure a mix.

## 8.6 Interview guide — citizens (45–60 min)

Run the interview in the participant's preferred language (Derja, MSA or French). The questions below are the core. Probe with "Tell me more…", "Can you show me…?", "What happened next?"

| Story (15') | Walk me through it from the moment you knew you needed it. Where did you find information? Who helped you? | Racontez-moi depuis le moment où vous avez su que vous en aviez besoin. ## 8.7 Interview guide — civil servants (45 min, FR/AR)

1. Describe a typical day. How do requests arrive, and how do you decide what to handle first?
2. What makes a file incomplete? How often? What happens then?
3. How do citizens ask about their file's status? How much of your time does that take?
4. Which tools do you use (paper, registers, software)? What works, and what doesn't?
5. How are deadlines tracked? Who sees whether they are met?
6. How do you verify documents? What fraud do you see?
7. What would make your work easier tomorrow? What would make it harder?
8. Who decides on new tools in your office? How are they procured?
9. *(Never ask to see real citizen files.)*

## 8.8 B2B discovery guide (45 min, FR/EN)

1. Describe your current remote onboarding / verification process, end to end.
2. How does BCT Circular 2025-06 (or 2026-10 for payment institutions) affect you? What evidence do auditors ask for?
3. What are your volumes, pass rates, abandonment and fraud rates (ranges are fine)?
4. Which vendors do you use? Where is data processed and stored?
5. Would a Tunisia-hosted orchestration with TunTrust e-signature and evidence packs be valuable? What would it replace?
6. How do you buy (budget owner, procurement steps, timeline)? What price model do you prefer?
7. Would you consider a paid pilot within 6 months? Under which conditions?

## 8.9 Usability test script (prototype)

Use the published prototype (private link: share it from the page's Share menu before sending it to participants), or the local `design/prototype/index.html`.
- **Setup:** ask the participant to think aloud. Explain: "We're testing the design, not you. Nothing here is real or official."

| # | Task | Start | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "Find out what documents you need for a residence certificate." | Home | Reaches Guide or service page and states both documents |
| T2 | "Ask the Guide the same question in the way you'd naturally write it." | Guide | Types a question (any language), understands the demo response and sources |
| T3 | "Your request needs something from you. Find out what and fix it." | Home | Opens request, uploads proof of address, sees status return to "In review" |
| T4 | "Book an appointment for tomorrow morning, then cancel it." | Request | Books then cancels |
| T5 | "Find who has looked at your data." | Home | Opens privacy center/access log and explains one entry |
| T6 | "Stop sharing your diploma with the employer." | Home | Withdraws consent c2 |
| T7 | "Your father asked you to help with his reimbursements. Switch to acting for him, then back to yourself." | Home | Uses acting switch; notices banner; switches back |
| T8 (officers) | "Process request REQ-2026-00412." | Officer workspace | Identifies missing document; requests correction or approves once complete |

**After the tasks:**
- SUS (Section 8.10).
- 3 open questions: what was confusing, what they'd trust, and whether they'd use this.
- Measures: task success (yes / with help / no), time on task, errors, SUS, and quotes.

## 8.10 System Usability Scale (SUS)

Use a **validated translation** where available. Validated French and Arabic SUS versions exist in the literature [A — confirm references before use]. Otherwise translate and back-translate. Scale 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the system was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the system.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

**Scoring:**
- Odd items: score − 1.
- Even items: 5 − score.
- Sum × 2.5.
- Phase 1 target ≥ 70; MVP target ≥ 75.

## 8.12 First-product discovery module — Workspace, Regulated Finance (added in v2.0)

**Objective:** validate or reject the first-product recommendation ([10](../10-first-product-recommendation.md)) with ≥ 15 financial institutions within 60 days.

### Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Confirm if | Refute if |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-R1 | Complaint handling under BCT 2022-08 is a recurring operational pain | ≥ 8 of 15 describe manual work, missed deadlines or report pain | Most report mature, satisfactory tooling |
| H-R2 | Payment providers and specialised FIs lack dedicated complaint tooling | ≥ 3 of 5 PSPs/FIs use email or spreadsheets | Already equipped |
| H-R3 | Institutions value Tunisian hosting / on-prem and Arabic/French templates enough to switch or buy | ≥ 5 rate these "must-have" | Indifferent |
| H-R4 | Willingness to pay matches tiers in [19](../19-commercial-and-pricing-strategy.md) | Stated budgets overlap tier ranges | Budgets far below |
| H-R5 | Design partners will join a paid sandbox programme | ≥ 2 sign | None |

### Screener (organisations)

1. Type: bank / financial institution / payment provider / microfinance / insurer.
2. Interviewee role: customer relations, complaints, compliance, IT.
3. Approximate monthly complaint volume.
4. Current tool, if any.

**Exclude** competitors and consultants selling competing tools.

### Interview guide (45 min, FR/AR/EN)

1. Walk me through the life of a complaint, from receipt to closure. Where does it get stuck?
2. How do you track deadlines? What share closes on time? How do you know?
3. How do you write and approve rejection reasons? Who checks consistency?
4. How do customers learn the status of their complaint?
5. What happens when a case goes to the banking mediator? How long does it take to compile the file?
6. How do you prepare the annual report to the board? How long does it take?
7. Which tools do you use (CRM, ticketing, core-banking module, spreadsheets)? What do you like or dislike?
8. What hosting or security requirements would a vendor need to meet (hosting location, on-premises, audits)?
9. Who would decide to buy a solution, and how (budget, procurement steps, timing)?
10. Would you join a 3-month sandbox design-partner programme (no production data) to shape the product?

### Ethics

- Do not collect real complaint content or customer personal data in interviews.
- Anonymised examples only, and only with the institution's consent.
