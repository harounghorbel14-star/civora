## Proposal to [Institution name]

### Faster, clearer and trackable public services: a pilot of the CIVORA service-delivery platform

**Submitted by:** CIVORA [legal entity name, registration no.] — an independent Tunisian technology company
**Date:** [date] · **Contact:** [name, role, phone]

### 1. Purpose

CIVORA proposes a **time-limited pilot** (3–6 months) to help [Institution] reduce processing times, repeat visits and "where is my file?" enquiries for **[1–3 selected services]**. The pilot deploys on [Institution]'s designated infrastructure and works with the national digital programmes, not in competition with them.

### 2. Context

- Tunisia has made strong progress on digital government:
  - a mobile digital identity [P]
  - the *e-Bawaba* portal and the *Khadamet* app [P]
  - a national interoperability platform with mandatory data exchange between administrations [P]
  - a national programme to simplify about 3,200 procedures (*Dalil*) [P]
- Citizens and officers still face three practical gaps in many offices:
  1. **Information:** citizens arrive without the right documents, so files are incomplete.
  2. **Status visibility:** citizens cannot see where their file is, so calls and visits multiply.
  3. **Back-office tooling:** officers lack simple queues, checklists and deadline tracking.

### 3. What we propose

| Component | What it does for [Institution] |
|---|---|
| **CIVORA Workspace** | Digital queue, document checklists, deadline (SLA) tracking, standard decisions with reasons, appointment management, supervisor dashboard |
| **Citizen request & tracking** (white-label, under [Institution]'s brand) | Citizens submit complete requests, track status and receive notifications in Arabic or French |
| **CIVORA Guide** (optional) | Multilingual assistant answering *only* from [Institution]'s official procedure content, with sources shown |
| **Consent & access log** | Every access to a citizen's data is logged and visible to the citizen. This supports compliance with personal-data protection obligations |

**Integration principle:**
- CIVORA does not access any national registry on its own.
- Any data exchange happens **only through [Institution]'s own authorised channels** (e.g. the national interoperability platform), under [Institution]'s control and agreements.

| Item | Proposal |

### 5. Expected results (to be baselined in month 1)

| Indicator | Target |
|---|---|
| Median processing time | −30% |
| Files requiring a second visit | −40% |
| Status enquiries (calls/visits) | −40% |
| Citizen satisfaction (digital channel) | ≥ 80% satisfied |
| Availability | ≥ 99.5% during office hours |
| Data incidents | Zero |

### 6. Data protection and security

- [Institution] remains **data controller**. CIVORA acts as **processor** under a written agreement.
- A privacy impact assessment (DPIA) will be prepared with [Institution]'s DPO. The INPDP formalities are completed before go-live.
- An independent security audit (ANCS-compliant) and penetration test take place before go-live.
- No data leaves Tunisia. No data is used for any other purpose. No advertising or data sale (CIVORA Citizen Data Charter).

### 7. What we ask from [Institution]

1. A sponsor (director level) and a day-to-day contact.
2. Access to official procedure content for the selected services, plus validation of that content.
3. Designation of hosting infrastructure and IT contact.
4. Participation of [n] officers in short training and feedback sessions.
5. A pilot agreement (convention) defining scope, roles, data protection and exit.

### 8. Governance

- A monthly steering committee (sponsor, CIVORA lead, IT, DPO).
- A weekly operational check-in.
- A change-control log.
- Joint communication: no public statement without [Institution]'s approval.

### 10. Exit and continuity

- At the end of the pilot, [Institution] may continue under a procured contract, or stop.
- If it stops, all data is returned in open formats and deleted from CIVORA-operated components, with a certificate of deletion.
