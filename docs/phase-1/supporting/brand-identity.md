# Brand Identity (concept v0.1) — with name investigation (Nov 2026)

> **Status: concept only.** Do not spend on brand production, domains or merchandise until **D12** (trademark clearance and the naming issue below) is decided.

## 6.1 Naming finding — requires a founder decision (D12)

**"CIVORA" collides with an existing brand in Arabic.**

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The natural Arabic transliteration of "Civora" is **سيفورا** (*sī-fūrā*), because Arabic has no /v/ and "v" is usually written ف | Standard transliteration practice [A] |
| **سيفورا** is the Arabic brand name used by **Sephora** on its own Middle East websites (e.g. sephora.me Arabic storefronts) | [V] [sephora.me Kuwait (Arabic)](https://www.sephora.me/kw-ar/); [sephora.me Bahrain terms (Arabic)](https://www.sephora.me/bh-ar/terms-publication) |
| The alternative letter ڤ is read as /g/ in Tunisia (e.g. ڤابس, Gabès), so "سيڤورا" would be read "Sigora" | [A] |

**Consequences:**
- Arabic-speaking citizens may hear or read the name as a cosmetics brand. This hurts a trust-centred public-service brand.
- It raises trademark risk in Arabic-script markets (GCC, Maghreb).
- No trademark search for "CIVORA" itself has been performed yet [U].

**Options:**

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep CIVORA, Latin-only wordmark** | Never transliterate. In Arabic contexts use the Latin logo plus a descriptive Arabic line, e.g. *منصّة خدمات المواطن* | No rename cost; works in FR/EN | Spoken Arabic will still say "سيفورا"; partial fix |
| **B. Keep CIVORA, distinct Arabic name** | Pair with an independent Arabic brand name with its own meaning (to be created and cleared) | Clear in Arabic; avoids collision | Two names to build; must clear both |
| **C. Rename before any brand investment** | Choose a new name pronounceable in AR/FR/EN with no collisions | Cleanest long-term | Delay; loss of any existing equity (minimal today) |

> **Updated recommendation (founder-requested investigation, 18 Nov 2026):** meaningful conflicts were found: an existing registered CIVORA mark in the public-sector data-platform field in Europe, plus the Arabic collision with Sephora. **Option C (rename) is now recommended before any significant branding expenditure.** "CIVORA" remains an internal working name until decision P1-D13. See §6.7.

**Original recommendation (v1.0):**

## 6.3 Logo concept — "the open C"

- **Mark:** a rounded-square tile in Civora Teal (`#0F5551`) holding a white open **C**, which embraces a jasmine-gold dot (`#F2C25C`). The dot stands for the citizen at the centre of service. The opening suggests an open door.
- **Files:**
  - [`design/brand/civora-mark.svg`](../../../design/brand/civora-mark.svg)
  - [`design/brand/civora-logo-horizontal.svg`](../../../design/brand/civora-logo-horizontal.svg)
- **Rules:**
  - The mark never mirrors in RTL.
  - Minimum size 24 px (mark) / 96 px wide (horizontal).
  - Clear space equals the dot diameter × 2.
- **Never combine with** national flags, coats of arms, the national red, crescents/stars, ministry logos, or the words "officiel", "national", "رسمي" or "وطني" in a way that suggests state status.

## 6.4 Colour rationale

| Colour | Why |
|---|---|
| Teal (primary) | Signals trust and calm. Distinct from the red-and-white national palette, so the brand never looks like an official state channel. Strong contrast on white (8.61:1) |
| Jasmine gold (accent) | A warm Mediterranean accent (jasmine is a widely recognised Tunisian cultural motif) used sparingly. Reserved for highlights and the synthetic-data banner |
| Slate neutrals | Readable, low-glare surfaces for long forms |

## 6.5 Brand voice (summary; details in Section 7)

- **Speak like a capable, kind counter agent:** direct, respectful, never condescending.
- **Explain the "why" briefly** when asking for data.
- **Admit uncertainty** ("We're not sure. Here's the official source.").
- **No hype, no exclamation marks** in service contexts.

## 6.7 Name investigation (founder request, 18 Nov 2026)

**Method and limits:**
- This is desk screening only: web search and trademark aggregators.
- Official registers could not be queried from the research environment: INNORPI (Tunisia), EUIPO/DPMA, WIPO Global Brand Database and USPTO TSDR.
- Domain registries (RDAP/WHOIS/DNS) were blocked by the environment's network policy.
- **Formal clearance by a qualified trademark attorney is required** (evidence E-11).

**Assessment: conflicts are meaningful.**
1. A prior registered **CIVORA** in the *same field* (public-sector and municipal data platforms) in the EU, a target market. This creates a high risk of opposition or infringement claims for software and government services in Europe, and possibly beyond through international extensions [A — attorney to confirm scope and classes].
2. The Arabic rendering collides with **Sephora**, a well-known mark. Expect consumer confusion in Arabic markets and an unprofessional association for a public-trust brand.

**Recommendation:** rename before significant branding expenditure.

**Rejected during ideation, with reasons:**
- **Taysir:** an existing Tunisian microfinance brand.
- **Wasl:** an Elm platform name in Saudi Arabia.
- **Arkan:** associated with a Serbian paramilitary leader.
- **Sitara:** Arabic ستارة means "curtain".
- **Tanora:** Arabic تنورة means "skirt".
- **Rafida:** close to an Arabic sectarian pejorative.
- **Masira:** political-media connotation.
- **Daraja:** an existing API product name (Safaricom).
- **Idara:** too close to idaraty.tn.
- **Nahda:** political-party association.

**Proposed shortlist for formal clearance:**
- **KELORA**
- **TELORA** (invented, language-neutral, no /v/ /p/ /g/ sounds, identical in Arabic, French and English)
- **NASAQ**, if the founder prefers an Arabic-meaning name
