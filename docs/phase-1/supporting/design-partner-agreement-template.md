## Sandbox Design-Partner Agreement

**Between:**
- [CIVORA legal entity, form, registered office, RNE number], represented by [name, title] ("**CIVORA**")
- [Partner institution, form, registered office, licence reference], represented by [name, title] ("**the Partner**")

**Date:** [ ] · **Reference:** [DP-2026-xx]

### 1. Purpose

1.1 The Partner agrees to help CIVORA design and evaluate *CIVORA Workspace — Regulated Finance* (the "**Product**"), a complaint and customer-request case-management solution, during a **sandbox evaluation**.

1.2 This Agreement does **not** cover any production use of the Product. Production use, if any, requires a separate commercial agreement, and is planned only in CIVORA's Phase 13 pilot deployment.

1.3 Nothing in this Agreement constitutes or implies endorsement by the Partner, the Banque Centrale de Tunisie or any public authority.

### 2. Duration

2.1 [3] months from [start date], extendable once by written agreement for up to [3] months.

### 3. Sandbox environment and data rules

3.1 The evaluation takes place in a CIVORA-operated **sandbox environment** hosted in Tunisia [hosting provider], or on infrastructure designated by the Partner.

3.2 **Permitted data:**
- (a) synthetic data generated by CIVORA or the Partner
- (b) **properly authorised test data**, meaning data the Partner confirms in writing it is entitled to provide for testing, which contains no personal data, or personal data that has been anonymised to a standard approved by the Partner's DPO and, where required, by counsel and the INPDP

3.3 **Prohibited:**
- real customer complaints or customer personal data, unless 3.2(b) is satisfied
- banking-secrecy-protected information
- production credentials
- connection to the Partner's production systems

3.4 Each party appoints a data contact. Any incident involving data is notified to the other party within [24] hours.

3.5 At the end of the Agreement, CIVORA deletes all Partner-provided test data within [15] days and confirms deletion in writing.

### 4. Partner contributions

The Partner will:
- (a) nominate a sponsor and up to [5] participants (e.g. complaints handlers, head of customer relations, compliance, IT)
- (b) take part in [4–6] working sessions and [2] usability tests
- (c) share non-confidential descriptions of its complaint-handling process, categories, templates and reporting needs
- (d) provide written feedback at the end of the evaluation

### 5. CIVORA contributions

CIVORA will:
- (a) provide sandbox access and support
- (b) configure the Product with the Partner's categories, deadlines and templates (test versions)
- (c) deliver a short evaluation report: findings, fit and gaps, indicative implementation plan
- (d) provide security documentation reasonably requested by the Partner's IT and compliance teams

### 6. Fees

6.1 Evaluation fee: **[amount] TND excl. VAT** (experimental pricing, P1-D9), payable [on signature / in two instalments].

6.2 If the Partner signs a commercial agreement within [6] months of the end of this Agreement, the evaluation fee is **credited** against the first year's subscription.

6.3 *[Alternative for counsel to consider: no fee, with a letter of intent instead.]*

### 7. Confidentiality

7.1 Each party keeps the other's confidential information confidential for [3] years after the end of the Agreement. This includes the Partner's processes and CIVORA's product roadmap and non-public materials.

7.2 Standard exclusions apply: public information, independently developed information, legally compelled disclosure.

### 8. Intellectual property and feedback

8.1 CIVORA retains all rights in the Product, including improvements made during the evaluation.

8.2 The Partner grants CIVORA a royalty-free right to use its **feedback** to improve the Product. Feedback is used without identifying the Partner and without using its confidential information.

8.3 The Partner retains all rights in its own data, templates and documents.

### 9. Publicity

Neither party may name the other or refer to this Agreement publicly (including in marketing, investor materials or case studies) without prior written consent. CIVORA must describe the relationship accurately, e.g. "design partner in a sandbox evaluation", and never as a deployment, endorsement or regulatory approval.

### 10. Security and compliance

10.1 CIVORA applies the security measures described in its security documentation, including access control, encryption, logging and in-country hosting.

10.2 The Partner may request reasonable evidence of these measures.

10.3 The parties acknowledge that this Agreement involves **no outsourcing of regulated activities** and no production processing.

*[Counsel to confirm against BCT outsourcing/IT-risk rules — question Q13.]*

### 11. Liability

11.1 The Product is provided for evaluation "as is". CIVORA's total liability is capped at the fees paid under this Agreement, except for breach of confidentiality, data obligations, or wilful misconduct.

11.2 Neither party is liable for indirect losses.

*[Counsel to adapt to Tunisian law.]*

### 12. Termination

Either party may terminate with [15] days' written notice. Clauses 3.5, 7, 8, 9 and 11 survive termination.

### 13. Governing law and disputes

Tunisian law. Amicable settlement first; failing that, [competent courts of Tunis / arbitration — counsel to advise].

## Points for counsel review

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | Is a fee-bearing sandbox evaluation compatible with the partner's procurement rules? Is a lighter LOI preferable for banks? |
| 2 | What anonymisation standard satisfies Law 2004-63 and banking secrecy for "properly authorised test data" (Phase 1 Q18)? |
| 3 | Does a sandbox evaluation by a bank or payment institution require any BCT notification (Q13)? See also question 7 |
| 4 | What liability cap and dispute-resolution forum are appropriate? |
| 5 | Is a French version required, plus an Arabic version for public bodies? |
| 6 | Is the feedback licence and IP clause enforceable as drafted? |
| 7 | *(Added 18 Nov 2026 after reading Law 2016-48.)* Art. 81 requires a bank or financial institution to **inform the BCT before signing an outsourcing contract**. One month of BCT silence counts as acceptance. Does a **sandbox** evaluation with synthetic data fall outside "externalisation"? Does a later production contract fall inside it? |
| 8 | *(Added from Law 2004-63.)* Art. 22 sets nationality, residency and no-criminal-record conditions for processors **and their agents**. Art. 52 makes INPDP authorisation mandatory for any transfer abroad. How should clause 3 (data rules) and clause 10 (security) reflect these if authorised test data contains any personal data? |
| 9 | *(Added from Law 2016-48 Art. 61.)* Should clause 7 (confidentiality) refer expressly to banking professional secrecy and Penal Code Art. 254? |
| 10 | Should clause 10 grant the partner, and the BCT on request, an **audit and access right**? Circular 2025-06 Art. 15 requires this of e-KYC providers, and banks are likely to ask for it by analogy |
